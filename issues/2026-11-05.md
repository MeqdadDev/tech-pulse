# TECH PULSE — NOV 03–05, 2026

> DeepSeek closed a record $15.9 billion round at a $90 billion valuation — the largest-ever funding deal for a Chinese startup — while Agility Robotics' SPAC filings went public ahead of taking its Digit humanoid robot business onto Nasdaq in a $2.5 billion merger.

## AI

- **[Moonshot AI ships Kimi K3.1 Pro's open weights](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)** — China's Moonshot released the open-weight version of Kimi K3.1 Pro on Nov. 4, a model landing just a few benchmark points behind the top closed frontier models. It's one of the most capable openly downloadable, self-hostable models yet, raising the bar for open-weight competition with US and Chinese rivals alike.

- **[Uber says almost no one actually uses AI agents to book rides](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/almost-no-one-uses-ai-agents-to-book-their-uber-company-says/)** — Despite ride-hailing being pitched as a flagship "agentic AI" use case, Uber reports real-world adoption is minimal. A useful reality check on how far agentic AI still is from everyday consumer use, even as investment in the category keeps surging (see Startups, below).

## Robotics

- **[Agility Robotics' SPAC filings reveal its finances ahead of a Nasdaq debut](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/digit-maker-agility-robotics-to-go-public-in-2-5b-deal-heres-what-the-filings-say-about-its-finances/)** — Newly public filings for Agility's $2.5B merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI show more than $620M in cash coming in ($420M from Churchill, roughly $200M from Foxconn-led investors) to scale its Digit humanoid warehouse robot, which already counts Schaeffler, GXO, Toyota, and Mercado Libre as customers, plus a single 1,000-unit, multi-year order. It's the clearest sign yet that humanoid robotics is moving from demo reels to public markets and real industrial contracts.

Coverage was otherwise thin for robotics this window.

## Software / Dev Tools

- **[Meta acquires AI coding startup Conductor](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/)** — Meta bought Conductor, whose tool lets engineers run multiple coding agents in parallel across isolated workspaces, folding another "agent orchestration for software engineering" company into a major platform. Conductor's standalone cloud service shuts down Nov. 20.

Coverage was otherwise thin for dev tools this window.

## Startups

- **[DeepSeek closes the largest-ever VC round for a Chinese startup](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/)** — DeepSeek raised $15.9B at a $90B valuation, the single biggest factor pushing global venture funding to $59B in October (up 45% year-over-year). AI-related companies took roughly two-thirds of all venture dollars that month.

- **[CrowdStrike buys two AI-security startups for nearly $500M](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/crowdstrike-to-shell-out-nearly-500m-for-2-ai-security-startups-as-the-market-for-securing-agents-takes-off/)** — CrowdStrike is acquiring Fiddler AI (AI agent observability, ~$400M) and Dreadnode (automated AI red-teaming, ~$95M), folding them into its Falcon platform as Falcon Observe and Falcon Guardian — a sign that "securing AI agents" is fast becoming its own cybersecurity category.

- **[AI inference host Fal doubles its valuation to $16B](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/ai-inference-startup-fal-doubles-its-valuation-to-16b-as-demand-surges/)** — Fal raised a $1.3B Series F led by Bessemer (with Nvidia and T. Rowe Price joining), doubling its valuation from $8B in April as demand for hosted AI video, image, and audio inference keeps surging; the company has already topped $800M in annualized revenue.

## Hardware

- **[AMD set to outsell Intel in a quarter for the first time in its history](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-expects-to-outsell-intel-in-q4-for-the-first-time-in-history-usd17b-guidance-would-exceed-intels-projections)** — AMD guided to roughly $17B in Q4 revenue, above Intel's own ~$15.5B midpoint projection, as the AI compute boom reshapes a four-decade rivalry.

- **[Nothing's CEO confirms its "AI-first" device is coming next month](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/nothing-ceo-says-companys-mysterious-ai-first-device-is-dropping-next-month/)** — Carl Pei confirmed the company's long-teased AI-native hardware will ship next month; rumors point to a wearable rather than a phone, part of a wider consumer-hardware push to build devices around AI assistants.
